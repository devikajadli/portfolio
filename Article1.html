The Entropy of Hustle Culture

Often, my mind revisits and readdresses the second law of thermodynamics, and I can’t seem to get it out of my head. That any spontaneously occurring process will always lead to an escalation in the entropy of the universe. The social media platform giants have become a separate universe in themselves and this law is a real-time live example of this. With the new trends like 75 HARD and constantly trying to become the it-girl, I have too, bowed down to the temptations, or a better way to put it, the matters of relevance in these times. I too started visualizing my own “dream life”, in a pretty, cute room without a speck of tiny dust and fake or real plants at different corners of the room to make my surroundings look prettier and of course, “aesthetic” along with state of the art yet minimalistic gadgets and equipment. There is, of course, no wrong doing, in wanting a “better” life and I do register the fact that these trends are undeniably encouraging “self-improvement” like waking up at 5 AM, working out, eating right, being organized and disciplined, travelling, socializing, caring for family and friends, studying, working 8 hours to earn, cooking, washing, cleaning, taking out time for skills you want to develop like learning a new instrument or language, while also pursuing your hobbies of self-interest, ALL WHILE DOCUMENTING THEM and intruding a safe space made for recreation!

I think, by now, we all have come to an agreement, that the idea of perfectionism is an unrealistic one, and we all have made ‘time-tables’ rationing our energy and time to a set of tasks bargaining and arguing with time for ourselves all this while. I sometimes speculate whether this is the idea of happiness?

Are we some over the top super duper ultra energized robots? What about rest and sleep and those little parts of the day where you just sit and do nothing? We don't read and write poetry because it's cute. We read and write poetry because we are members of the human race. And the human race is filled with passion. And medicine, law, business, engineering, these are noble pursuits and necessary to sustain life. But poetry, beauty, romance, love, these are what we stay alive for. That’s right, I did quote Dead Poet’s Society, as cliché as it sounds!

I am not here to contradict the fact that a sense of purpose fuels our existence. But then is ‘hustling’ and keeping up with Instagram trends to avoid fomo really promoting happiness?

From the inception of time, humans are yet to understand the “real world” and the know-hows of living it. We all could easily agree it would only be fair to not come up with a pressurizing virtual world to figure out to live in.
